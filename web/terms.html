What QuoPull is

QuoPull is an onchain experiment on Robinhood Chain. It has four parts: the Art Pass, a fixed supply NFT; QPULL, a token that trades in one locked liquidity pool; a trade tax that funds three prize games (a daily pack raffle, a weekly holder draw and a weekly leaderboard); and QUOTRON, a separate token made by a third party, in which prizes are paid.

The rules of QuoPull are the smart contract code. This site and our documentation describe that code in plain language, but they can be incomplete, out of date or wrong. If the site and the contracts disagree, the contracts decide what happens.

Prize games and chance

  • Winners are picked by the contracts using public randomness from Drand and the rules in the code. We cannot choose, predict in advance or change winners.
  • Winning is never guaranteed. The chance of winning depends on how many entries there are, and most entries do not win.

  • You must claim a prize yourself, within its claim window (currently 30 days). Unclaimed prizes return to the prize pot and are lost to you.

07 / Terms

Prices, tax and fees

  • The mint. Art Passes cost the mint price shown in the contract. Proceeds split 80% to the liquidity pool, 10% to the prize seed and 10% to team + operations.
  • Trade tax. Every QPULL trade in the pool pays a tax, taken in WETH: 4% on buys, and on sells a higher launch rate that steps down to 4% over the first 48 hours. The tax funds the prize games (80%) and team + operations (20%).
  • Network fees. Every transaction costs gas, even one that fails, is cancelled by the contract or loses a race with another transaction.
  • Your taxes. You are responsible for any tax on your activity, including on prizes, trades and mints.
08 / Terms

Launch rules

  • For the first hour after the pool opens, only wallets holding an Art Pass can buy, each buy is capped, and there is a short wait between buys. A wallet that buys in that hour cannot transfer its passes out until the hour ends.
  • These limits are enforced by the contracts on the wallet that signs the transaction. A pass held in a different wallet, a vault or a smart contract wallet does not count, and purchases through relayers or smart contract wallets may be credited to someone else.
  • The contracts can refuse a transaction for many reasons: a limit, a price move, a closed window, too little gas. A refused transaction still costs gas.
